# October 28 account-access outage

Production account access was restored at 20:11:50 UTC (1:11:50 p.m. Pacific) using the guarded original-primary recovery procedure. This is incident recovery, not a permanent fix or evidence that the availability objective has been achieved.

## Evidence and timeline

- 09:46:23 UTC: the existing incident investigation recorded connection retries, followed at 09:46:24 by capacity retries. The deployed classifier maps the capacity code exclusively to PostgreSQL SQLSTATE 53300. These are retry counts, not affected-member counts.
- 09:49:23 UTC: quarantine errors appeared. Four operations had no completion receipts. No intervening deployment was identified. The exact excess-connection mechanism and individual SQL commit outcomes remain unproven.
- 20:07 UTC: external requests still received `PRIVACY_CHECKPOINT_REQUIRED` on anonymous session lookup while public `/health` returned 200. The monitor reported 621 failed readiness minutes. This confirmed an account-access outage, not a shell outage.
- 20:08 UTC: read-only inspection found the original primary, unchanged routing, matching SQL/journal generations and sequences, no outstanding cleanup, and no active or idle-in-transaction writers. Queue delivery was paused without purging messages or removing its consumer. Evidence was collected again after at least two minutes of writer drain.
- 20:11:50 UTC: guarded recovery completed. It verified the independent chain, 18 surviving media objects and unchanged capability fingerprints across 27 tables; resolved four pending intents; conditionally activated the journal; and verified removal of the temporary operator Worker/domain. It performed no SQL mutation or session/key reset.
- Queue delivery was restored to its previous settings. The independent post-recovery observation confirmed that capability fingerprints still matched the pre-recovery snapshot.
- 20:12–20:13 UTC: both `.org` and `.com` passed anonymous session and protected operations checks. An isolated browser passed password login, 24 authorized bootstrap/feed reads, logout and rejection of the old cookie with 401. The test identity was removed and absence verified. No real-member account was used.
- 20:15:18 UTC: both domains still passed protected health 130 seconds after completion of the browser acceptance check, beyond the stale-intent threshold. Monitoring had returned to operational with all 625 failed minute observations retained. Independent provider reconciliation matched the canonical six-Worker deployment, domains, queues and schedules.
- 20:13 UTC: a read-only connection snapshot found 25 maximum connections, three superuser-reserved slots, seven idle auth connections, five idle social connections, five idle jobs connections and the observer. This snapshot does not reconstruct peak incident allocation or certify load capacity.

The recorded quarantine-error-to-recovery interval is approximately 10 hours 22 minutes. This estimate uses the first recorded quarantine error and recovery completion; it is not a precise per-member outage duration. Preserve the monitor's failed observations and coverage gaps.

## Cause and remaining work

Connection exhaustion preceded unresolved journal operations and the global privacy quarantine. This causal sequence is supported by fixed-code telemetry and independent control inspection. Why the connection pools exhausted capacity, and why each operation lost completion, still require investigation. All three Hyperdrive configurations currently connect directly to PostgreSQL; their configured pool limits are soft. Current retries do not solve sustained overload or durable process loss.

The public health endpoint cannot detect this class of outage. Protected readiness detected it, but detection did not produce timely recovery. Monitoring also shares the application's Cloudflare failure domain. The stricter [99.99% reliability plan](reliability.md) covers connection admission, durable completion, external journey monitoring, alert escalation and deployment recovery. These are outstanding implementation/verification requirements.

Recovery used the existing executable and configuration: production Worker `44afbb8d-3133-4aac-8663-efd6fb5feb47`, runtime source `005454607c98ffdb7c430a947bedb4586a14a45b`. No migration, new application deployment, domain change or signup-policy change occurred. The recovery suite passed all 17 tests against disposable local PostgreSQL; private-file tests also passed. Restricted evidence is in the owner-only `artifacts/private/restore-ops/reliability-20261028/` and `artifacts/private/reliability-20261028/` directories, excluded from source control.
