# Larger and longer private videos

Implemented and enabled in staging and production on November 6, 2026. [Release evidence](../docs/evidence/larger-videos-release-2026-11-06.md) records exact sources, live acceptance and remaining physical-device/distribution verification under #108.

## Recommendation

Let people share a video up to **10 minutes**, selecting a source up to **1 GiB**, and prepare a private, broadly playable **1080p H.264/AAC** copy on their device. Cap the prepared upload at **250 MiB**. These are the initial product limits; further device memory, battery, codec and cost measurements may lead to adjustments. Offer trimming and a smaller 720p option before upload. Keep the original untouched on the person’s device; explain that Porchday stores the prepared copy, not an original-quality backup.

The experience should feel like sharing a photo: select, preview, optionally trim, choose audience, share. Show duration and approximate upload size. Use explicit Preparing / Uploading / Ready / Failed states, progress, Cancel and Retry. Publish only after all media and its encrypted manifest are durable. Never leave a broken public feed item when preparation or upload fails. A suspended mobile app may pause; show that honestly and resume when possible instead of promising background completion.

A video in the feed has an encrypted poster, duration and Play control. Playback begins only on request, starts before the entire video downloads, and supports seek, fullscreen, mute, captions when supplied, and accessible controls. Keep the same comments, reactions, audience, expiry and album semantics as other media. Do not add another top-level navigation destination or change the meaning of the current Photos filter in this release; start with video attachments in posts. Standalone videos and album placement need an explicit follow-up product decision.

## Constraints before implementation

`apps/web/src/video.ts` limits original MP4/MOV/WebM bytes to 10 MiB, validates decoding and 4K dimensions, and makes no conversion. The whole media envelope is downloaded/decrypted for playback. The API has a 22,020,096-byte envelope bound, and archives have their own per-part limits. Increasing a constant would create memory, upload, playback and portability failures. Existing small-video envelopes must remain readable.

## Architecture

1. **Prepare locally.** Inspect the real container and streams, finite duration, dimensions, rotation and audio. Normalize rotation, strip location/device metadata, and generate an encrypted poster. Select 1080p or 720p; never silently discard audio. Tone-map HDR explicitly and verify color/audio sync, or explain the unsupported source before upload. Decode/encode in bounded queues; do not load the whole 1 GiB source into memory. On Apple platforms use AVFoundation; web feasibility must cover demuxing, encoding and muxing, not just codec availability. Browser APIs expose different codec support, so feature-detect the exact configuration. If local preparation is unsupported, offer an already-compatible smaller file or the native app, with clear guidance; never upload plaintext for cloud transcoding.
2. **Versioned encrypted media.** Define a v2 media manifest containing codec/init data, duration, dimensions, segment index, ciphertext digests, lengths and timing, protected by the existing author signature and recipient key envelope. Encrypt independently addressable segments using a reviewed AEAD construction with unique nonces and authenticated binding to immutable media/version/segment identity. Bind ordering and completeness in the signed manifest. Do not hand-roll a replacement for the existing encryption protocol; publish test vectors and review the extension before implementation. Recipients verify integrity and context before passing bytes to a decoder.
3. **Resumable transfer.** Use bounded binary uploads of immutable encrypted chunks, initially targeting roughly 4 MiB (a transport target, not an R2 multipart part-size assumption). Start/resume/status/complete/abort operations have stable operation IDs and idempotent outcomes. Reserve declared storage before receiving bytes and enforce actual totals and chunk counts. Check ownership, quotas and authorization on every operation. Reconcile an uncertain completion instead of starting a second upload. Incomplete uploads expire after a proposed 24 hours and release quota; cancellation schedules durable cleanup. If object-store multipart is used underneath, conform separately to its minimum part sizes and completion/abort rules.
4. **Progressive playback.** Decrypt and validate only a bounded window of segments into the platform playback pipeline. Seeking fetches the needed segments. Do not expose a public plaintext playlist or transferable unauthenticated object URL. Check current content access on every segment request; stop and release buffers when access expires, is revoked, or the viewer closes. Previously downloaded copies cannot be recalled. Native and web must implement the same manifest and error semantics.
5. **Lifecycle and portability.** Include segments, manifests and posters in deletion, expiry, moderation holds, backup/restore and orphan reconciliation. Preserve the existing union of photo/album grants if videos later become first-class album media; album access must not remove prior grants. Archive v2 must stream/chunk large media across bounded parts, verify completeness/digests before import, and never silently omit a video. History sharing rewraps authorized keys; it must not duplicate/transcode media or broaden audiences. Older clients show a useful update-required placeholder while keeping post text readable.

The server can observe encrypted byte lengths, timing and recipient/delivery metadata. It cannot inspect duration, validate codec claims or transcode encrypted content; clients enforce duration/format limits, while the server enforces bytes, quotas and abuse limits. Don’t describe the duration limit as server-enforceable under end-to-end encryption.

## Capacity and alternatives

At a proposed average 3 Mb/s video plus 128 kb/s audio, ten minutes is about 224 MiB before container/encryption overhead. The 250 MiB ceiling still needs a budget-aware encoder; high-motion scenes may need lower quality or 720p. A ten-minute original can exceed 1 GiB and should receive trim/export guidance, not a crash. One hundred maximum-size uploads consume about 24.4 GiB before replicas/backups. Twenty complete plays of one maximum-size clip deliver about 4.9 GiB. Measure storage, request counts, backup amplification, playback bytes, preparation time and peak memory with synthetic clips before pricing or quota decisions; no unverified provider-cost estimate is assumed here.

Raising the current whole-file limit is a poor first step. Server-side transcoding would require access to plaintext and contradict the current privacy model. Uploading originals only is simpler but makes large downloads and codec failures the reader’s problem. Local preparation plus resumable encrypted segments best fits the product, with a larger implementation cost that should be planned explicitly.

## Delivery sequence and acceptance

1. Prototype the complete 1080p prepare/encrypt/upload/seek path with synthetic clips on Chromium, WebKit, installed PWA, iOS and macOS. Measure peak memory, battery/thermal behavior, codec gaps and time-to-first-frame. Confirm proposed limits from evidence.
2. Review the protocol/nonce design, manifest versioning, access checks and export/import format. Implement additive schema and old-client fallback. Build both native clients before production rollout.
3. Ship behind a default-off capability with small synthetic/member pilots and bounded quotas. Roll back publishing v2 while retaining its readers and deletion support; never strand uploaded media by rolling back to readers that cannot open it.
4. Enable only after interruption/resume, repeat completion, tampered/reordered/truncated chunks, quota races, seek, expiry/revocation, orphan cleanup, backup restore and cross-client archive tests pass. Test portrait/landscape, silent clips, iPhone HEVC/HDR, variable frame rate, long GOPs, malformed containers and unsupported codecs. Record device acceptance separately from automated CI.

Initial implementation choices: 10 minutes / 1 GiB source / 250 MiB prepared limits; preserve the existing 1 GiB per-member media quota; offer explicit 1080p and 720p choices with 1080p selected initially. Start with post attachments. Independent videos and video album placement remain outside this release. Device performance measurements may require lowering the initial limits, with evidence recorded before rollout.

## Platform references

- [WebCodecs specification](https://www.w3.org/TR/webcodecs/) and [Mozilla’s WebCodecs guide](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/WebCodecs_API): codec primitives do not provide a complete container/muxing pipeline; runtime configuration support must be checked.
- [Apple AVAssetExportSession export](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/avassetexportsession/export(to:as:isolation:)): local export supports cancellation and format checks.
- [Cloudflare R2 upload guidance](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/objects/upload-objects/) and [R2 limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/platform/limits/): application chunking and object-store multipart have separate constraints.

## Implemented protocol and rollout contract

Implementation branch `codex/larger-videos` uses a fast-start MP4 divided into fixed 4 MiB plaintext byte ranges (last range may be shorter). Timing/seek metadata stays inside the MP4; a separate plaintext playlist is never published. The signed OpenPGP media payload contains `videoVersion: 2` and a `video` manifest with protocol `porchday-video-aes256gcm-v2`, a random stream UUID, a 32-byte key, size/duration/dimensions, chunk size and ordered segment records. Each record carries its ciphertext length, SHA-256 and independently random 12-byte nonce. AES-256-GCM uses a 16-byte authentication tag and ASCII additional data `porchday-video-v2\n<stream UUID>\n<index>\n<plaintext length>`. The stream identity remains stable across audience-specific signed manifest rewraps. Neither the key nor nonce enters routing metadata, logs or the durable upload queue.

Migration 073 stores immutable stream specifications and chunk receipts separately from existing media grants. Chunk keys use the existing private `media/<owner>/<object>` namespace, allowing the deletion outbox, replay journal and bounded backup objects to retain their current format. Reservation serializes against the member profile and counts both existing media and reserved stream bytes against 1 GiB. Completion requires every declared chunk. A root cannot become ready before its stream completes, and v2 roots cannot become independent photo or album grants. Backup inventories include complete referenced streams; deletion replay denies access to restored objects that are already scheduled for deletion.

Web preparation uses pinned Mediabunny 1.65.2, loaded on demand. It remuxes compatible H.264/AAC sources or transcodes locally, writes temporary output through OPFS, and removes metadata. Native preparation uses file-backed Photos/Files selections and AVFoundation. HDR sources currently receive an explicit SDR-export instruction rather than unverified tone mapping. Browsers without working local storage/codecs receive native-app or compatible-export guidance. Temporary plaintext preparation files are separate from encrypted durable retry state.

The browser player serves authorized, decrypted ranges through a tab-bound service worker URL with no cache persistence. Native AVPlayer requests ranges through a resource loader. Both check chunk integrity and current API access, enforce expiry, and release buffers on close or app/tab backgrounding. The native implementation normalizes CryptoKit Data slice indices before exposing zero-based byte ranges; regression coverage includes corrupt/truncated/index-mismatched ciphertext.

Archive version 2 retains the existing 20 MiB target / 30 MiB maximum per-part bound. `video_segments` records carry independently verified plaintext pieces at `videos/<media ID>/<index>.part`; a single media record binds their order, sizes, hashes and complete-file SHA-256. Import requires every segment, verifies the complete file while assembling on local disk, and encrypts fresh chunks for the importing member. `videos.html` is a standalone offline viewer with an explicit folder picker; it verifies each bounded piece before playback. Exported files remain outside service deletion control. Version 1 archives remain readable.

Older clients receive a small compatible MP4 update card in the legacy `bytes` field of a v2 envelope. Updated readers ignore that card and stream the actual video. Export APIs require an explicit v2 capability for these roots, and history/reshare replacement checks prevent old clients from replacing a video with its update card. Existing post text remains readable.

`VIDEO_UPLOADS_V2` defaults off. Install and verify a reader-capable web/API baseline before enabling writers, then retain that baseline as the rollback target. Turning publishing off keeps reading, exports, expiry and deletion available. Release evidence must name exact web/API/native source, automated checks, staging acceptance and actual live versions. Physical-device codec, memory, battery and thermal checks remain recorded follow-ups in #108; they are not claimed by simulator or hosted-Mac tests.
